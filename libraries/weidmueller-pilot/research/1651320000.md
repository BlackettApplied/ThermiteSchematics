## Topology and scope

The five populated contacts are separate passive conductors between each screw termination and its corresponding female mating contact. The candidate declares **five fixed links**, `C.n–M.n` for n = 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. These are factory contact paths, not bridges between adjacent contacts. The coordinator's fixture must materialize the links as explicit jumpers: single-terminal functions and connector metadata do not join nets.

The remaining endpoint, **PE**, is one aggregate logical protective-earth connection. Exact data-sheet p.3 specifies an M4 PE screw connection; p.4 and the legacy diagram show PE-marked hardware. Those sources do **not** establish an inventory of all independent PE clamps and mating/bonding points or complete frame continuity. The model therefore does not assert exactly one PE screw, a central PE pin, a `C.PE–M.PE` bridge, a mounting-screw bond or a housing bond. The five numbered mating contacts appear in connector metadata; aggregate PE is kept separate rather than assigned an invented mating-pin position.

**Required PE is an explicit library policy for modeled protective-earth use**, not a universal manufacturer instruction for every possible use. An authored PE connection satisfies presence only; it does not demonstrate an external earth source or adequate bonding. Other contacts are application-dependent. There are eleven modeled endpoints in total.

The legacy p.1 phrase “lagging earth contacts” is not adopted as a delayed protective-earth claim. The exact modern data-sheet p.1 instead specifies **two lagging contacts**, and its p.3 separately counts two signal and three power contacts. B.82 distinguishes the two pilot/switch positions from power. Mating sequence and an external pilot/interlock circuit are outside this passive insert's internal topology; no relay, switch opening or pilot-to-pilot bridge is invented. Modern B.80 expressly prohibits connecting or disconnecting with live voltage or load.

## Capability and preparation discrepancies

Capabilities stay in notes, without terminal `nominal_voltage`, AC-only restrictions or scalar current:

- Exact data-sheet p.3: **830 V DIN EN 61984**, **600 V AC/DC UL/CSA**, **8 kV** impulse, pollution severity **3**. RockStar B.80 and legacy p.1 explicitly limit **signal contacts to 500 V**.
- **20/23/24 A conflict:** exact ordering/general data on pp.1/3 and RockStar B.80/B.82 say **20 A**. The same exact data-sheet's family introduction says **24 A**. Historical p.1 says **23 A with HVE 3+2 = 24 A**, and its size-4 p.2 heading says **24 A**. These edition/section differences remain visible; installed-edition applicability and conductor/pole/ambient derating are unresolved.- Main contact data: **M3, 0.5–0.55 Nm**, AWG20–14, nominal **0.5–2.5 mm²**. The exact data-sheet p.3 also gives **4 mm²** in several conductor-specific fields, so no universal 4 mm² preparation is asserted. Exact/legacy strip length is **9 mm**; modern B.80 says **8 mm**.
- PE data: **M4, 1.2–1.5 Nm**, **10 mm** strip, AWG20–12, rated **4 mm²** (exact p.3); B.80 gives a **0.5–4 mm²** range. These specifications do not establish the number of distinct PE connection points.
- The **−40…125 °C** range does not imply full-current operation across that range. Derating curves on B.82 depend on conductor, populated pole count and ambient temperature.

No choice of one conflicting figure is silently promoted into an electrical compatibility constraint.
