Casing and dot separators are authoring namespaces. `SHIELD` is a shell alias, not a numbered RJ45 pin or a fabricated PE clamp. These are IM socket contacts; cable plugs and separately ordered ET 200S terminal modules are outside the model.

Each new `ports` entry uses `medium: "ethernet"`, `connector: "RJ45"`, and `required: false`, consistent with the schema and existing Siemens CPU/HMI models. Physical `connectorPorts` use **X1.P1.CONTACTS** and **X1.P2.CONTACTS** to preserve the pin inventories. The compiler rejects an identical key in both port collections with **E207**. `CONTACTS` is therefore an explicit authoring suffix, not a printed marking. Each logical port and its physical pin-map alias describe the **same socket**, not extra hardware; do not author independent cables to both representations. The schema has no `protocol` property on a port; authored communication relations carry `protocol: "PROFINET"`. Communication metadata creates no physical net continuity.

P.13 specifies **100BASE-TX**, **100 Mbit/s full duplex for PROFINET IO**, **10 Mbit/s Ethernet services**, autonegotiation and autocrossing. P.8 confirms an active two-port switch and port disabling. Neither specific port is universally required; no false requirement to wire both is added. A functioning PROFINET application still needs its intended network path; the current schema cannot express an at-least-one-of-two port rule.

## Supply, links and grounding review

Table 1-1 explicitly names 2L+ and 2M as loop-through, supporting exactly **1L+–2L+** and **1M–2M** as fixed factory feed paths. Project jumpers represent those paths; they are not field jumper installation instructions. The rated active-module supply is **24 V DC** (p.14), so **1L+ and 1M remain required**. Feeding through 2L+/2M can satisfy this via the explicit fixed paths; the duplicates are not independently required. Shared connection policy does not claim clamp conductor capacity or loop-through ampacity.

The original grounding note was too broad. **P.7 explicitly documents an RC combination between supply M and the rail/protective conductor.** This is not a fixed wire and must not become a jumper. **P.14 documents Ethernet-to-electronics galvanic isolation**, with **no isolation** between electronics and the rear-panel bus or supply. Physical RC/rail endpoints and the detailed RJ45 GND/shell/rail/M matrix remain unknown. Matching GND names do not establish hard links, and missing modeled links must not imply isolation.

The p.13 block diagram has unexplained supply-side labels 2(P1)/6(P1)/3(P2)/7(P2). These do not override Table 1-1's explicit supply legends and are not applied to RJ45 pins. The diagram corroborates duplicate feed branches but does not resolve this labeling discrepancy.

P.14 also states approximately **200 mA** consumption, **3.3 W** dissipation, reverse-polarity protection, at least **20 ms** power-failure bypass, permitted potential difference to rail **75 V DC / 60 V AC**, and **500 V DC** insulation-test voltage. None establishes supply-loop ampacity, external bonding adequacy or an allowable input-voltage range.
