## Numbered inventory and contact roles

The female arrangement directly shows **1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 12, 14, 16**, plus PE. In that historical view, 1/3/5/7 are in the left column and 10/12/14/16 in the right, staggered from top to bottom. Do not fill unused body positions into a consecutive 1–16 inventory.

The two endmost distinct contacts are **1 and 16**. The newer female **screw** role diagram identifies two endmost switch contacts and six operating contacts. Their role comparison supports **pilots 1/16** and **power contacts 3/5/7/10/12/14**. This is explicitly a cross-diagram inference: old and new circle-fill conventions are opposite, and the modern female diagram places the lower switch in the other horizontal column from historical contact 16. Therefore the model asserts numbers and roles, but **no physical orientation, coordinates or phase map**. The adjacent tension-clamp diagram is not the screw diagram.

There are **17 authoring endpoints**: C.n and M.n for all eight numbered contacts, plus one PE. C. means cable screw termination and M. means the female mating interface of that same contact; prefixes are authoring aliases, not stamped markings. Eight explicit **C.n–M.n** fixed links represent within-insert factory copper continuity. No other poles, external mating half or pilot pair are joined. Lagging pilot contacts are not an internal relay or safety-rated load-breaking function.

**PE is a single required aggregate endpoint with partial coverage.** Exact PE clamp/mating/frame/housing sub-interfaces and their complete bonding matrix are not established. No C.PE/M.PE split or PE fixed link is authored. Required PE is explicit library policy for modeled protective-earth use, not a universal manufacturer installation quotation. Presence does not prove upstream bonding; external protective bonding must be established by the project, and deliberate non-use may be documented.

## Conditional ratings and unresolved differences

- Current exact technical/ordering tables and the 2020 catalogue say **20 A**. The historical exact size-6 page and current sheet introduction say **23 A**. Preserve this discrepancy; omit scalar current. It is not explained away as a supposed single-conductor value.
- Main voltage capability is **830 V** under DIN EN 61984; B.80 specifies **500 V for signal contacts**. UL/CSA gives **600 V AC/DC**, not an override of the signal qualification. No passive capability becomes terminal nominal_voltage.
- Published conditions include 8 kV impulse, pollution degree 3, silver-passivated copper alloy, contact resistance ≤2 mΩ, at least 500 silver-contact cycles and −40 to +125 °C limits. B.84's two derating plots distinguish **tension-clamp xT and screw xS**, with wire/pole-count conditions; they do not describe separately rated power/pilot circuits or guarantee full current at the hot endpoint.
- Ordinary screw data: M3, 0.5–0.55 Nm, nominal 0.5–2.5 mm² / AWG20–14. Current sheet says 9 mm strip and also contains 4 mm² solid/flexible subfields; 2020 B.80 says 8 mm strip. Preserve these differences rather than flattening them into scalar wiring limits. PE: M4, 4 mm² rated section / AWG20–12, 10 mm strip, 1.2–1.5 Nm.
- Do not connect/disconnect with live voltage or load. Historical language about lagging earth contacts is not evidence for delayed protective-earth operation. Housing/hood, mating compatibility, IP/sealing, installed revision and protective measures remain project work.
