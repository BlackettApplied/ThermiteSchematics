## Nature / function

A terminal shield is an **insulation accessory** (Schneider groups it under
"isolation/insulation accessories"). It is a moulded, non-conductive shroud that
covers the exposed power terminals of the host circuit breaker to prevent direct
contact and preserve the clearance/creepage distances required by IEC 60947-2.

Electrically it is **inert**: it has no terminals, no pins, no conductor path,
and no bonding point. It does **not** terminate, jumper, short or bond the host
breaker's poles, and it adds no rating of its own. Pole count ("3P") and any
current/voltage ratings belong to the **host** Compact NS100/160/250 breaker,
not to this cover; therefore no `rating` and no `nominal_voltage` are assigned to
this part.

## Modeling

Zero-terminal mechanical accessory, mirroring the reference `accessory.json`
(protective cap) pattern:

- `terminals: {}`, `functions: {}` — no electrical interface.
- One documentation `connectorPorts.COVER` describing the mechanical cover only.
- `connectionCoverage.status: complete` — an insulating cover has, by design, no
  electrical connections; the empty inventory is exhaustive, not merely unknown.
- `conformance`: no terminal keys, no required keys, **no fixed links** (a shield
  never joins nets), `renderKind: accessory`.
